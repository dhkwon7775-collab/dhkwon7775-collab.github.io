How the agent fee is calculated

Step by step

  1. Deal amount: purchase price, jeonse deposit, or for monthly rent: deposit + rent × 100 (× 70 if that is under ₩50 million).
  2. Find the rate cap for that amount in the table.
  3. Fee = deal amount × rate, up to the ceiling where one applies.

Good to know

  • Both sides pay their own agent. The rates are maximums, so agree on the fee before signing.
  • Fees jump at bracket edges (for example ₩900 million for buying). Near an edge, ask for the lower rate.
  • Ask for a cash receipt — it also counts in the year-end tax settlement.

FAQ

How much is the real estate agent fee for renting in Korea?

For monthly rent the deal amount is deposit + monthly rent × 100 (× 70 if that comes to under ₩50 million), and a rate cap of 0.3–0.6% applies. A ₩10 million deposit with ₩700,000 rent counts as ₩80 million: 0.4% would be ₩320,000, but the ceiling for ₩50–100 million is ₩300,000, so the most the tenant and the landlord each pay is ₩300,000.

Who pays the agent fee in Korea?

Both sides pay their own agent fee: the tenant and the landlord for a lease, the buyer and the seller for a purchase. If one agent handles both sides, each still pays up to the cap.

Can I negotiate the agent fee?

Yes. The legal rates are maximums. It is common to agree on the fee (and whether VAT is added) before signing the contract — ask for it in writing.

When do I pay the agent fee?

Usually on the move-in or balance payment day (잔금일), once the deal is completed. Ask for a receipt (cash receipt or tax invoice).
