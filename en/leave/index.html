Annual leave applies at workplaces with 5 or more employees. From year 1 you need 80% attendance in the previous year to get the full 15+ days.

How annual leave builds up

The rules

  • First year: 1 day for each full month worked, up to 11 days.
  • After 1 year (with 80%+ attendance): 15 days.
  • From year 3: 1 more day every 2 years, up to 25 days.
  • Unused leave pay = unused days × daily ordinary wage (monthly ÷ 209 × 8).

Good to know

  • Workplaces with fewer than 5 employees are not covered.
  • If you leave exactly on your 1-year anniversary, the 15 days are not created — you must still be employed the next day.
  • If the employer formally pushed you to use leave and you did not, unused leave pay may not be owed.

Based on: Labor Standards Act Art. 60 (annual paid leave), Art. 61 (leave-use promotion)

FAQ

How many days of annual leave do you get in Korea?

In the first year you earn 1 paid day for each full month worked (up to 11). After one year you get 15 days, and from the third year one more day every two years, up to 25 days. This applies to workplaces with 5 or more employees.

Do foreign workers get annual leave in Korea?

Yes. The Labor Standards Act applies regardless of nationality or visa, so E-9, E-7, D-10 and other workers get the same annual leave as Korean employees.

What happens to unused annual leave?

Leave not used within a year usually turns into unused leave pay (ordinary daily wage × unused days). If your employer formally asked you to use it (leave-use promotion) and you did not, the pay may not be owed. When you quit, unused leave must be paid out.

Are public holidays counted as annual leave?

No. Public holidays are separate paid days off at workplaces with 5 or more employees, so they are not taken from your annual leave.
