Korean Age Explained: International Age vs Korean Age After the 2023 Law

Korea Calculators · Updated 2026-10-25

In short
  • International age is now the official age in Korea (since June 28, 2023).
  • Korean age (1 at birth, +1 every New Year) is informal only.
  • Year age (this year − birth year) is still used for alcohol, tobacco and military service.

Three ways Koreans count age

NameHow it worksUsed for
International age 만 나이0 at birth, +1 on each birthdayLaws, contracts, medical care
Year age 연 나이Current year − birth yearAlcohol and tobacco, military service
Korean age 세는 나이1 at birth, +1 every January 1Casual conversation only

Example

For someone born on December 25, 1995, on October 22, 2026:

  • International age: 30 (birthday not yet passed this year)
  • Year age: 31
  • Korean age: 32

A baby born on December 31 used to turn "2" the next morning in Korean age — one reason the law was changed.

What changed in 2023

From June 28, 2023, ages in Korean laws, contracts and official documents mean international age unless a law says otherwise. A contract that says "60 or older" now means 60 in international age.

Where year age still applies

  • Alcohol and tobacco: you can buy them from January 1 of the year you turn 19, whatever your birthday.
  • Military service obligations follow year age.
  • School entry: children born in the same calendar year start school together.

Asking someone's age

Koreans often ask age to decide how politely to speak. Saying your birth year ("I was born in '98") is the clearest answer and avoids any confusion about which system you mean.

FAQ

Why do Koreans ask my age?

Age affects the level of politeness and titles people use with each other. It is usually just a way to get the relationship right, not rudeness.

Do Koreans still use Korean age?

Many people still use it casually, especially older generations, but official matters now use international age.
