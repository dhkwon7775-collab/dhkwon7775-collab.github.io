National Pension Refund for Foreigners Leaving Korea: Who Qualifies and How to Claim

Korea Calculators · Updated 2026-10-30

At a glance
  • When you leave Korea for good, you may get your pension contributions back as a lump-sum refund (반환일시금).
  • The refund includes your share and your employer's share, plus interest.
  • Eligibility depends on your nationality and, for some workers, your visa.
  • Claim within 5 years of leaving.

Who qualifies

You can usually get a refund if any one of these applies:

  1. Your home country gives Koreans the same kind of refund (reciprocity).
  2. Your country has a social security agreement with Korea that includes a refund.
  3. You joined on an E-8 (training employment), E-9 (non-professional employment) or H-2 (working visit) visa — these holders qualify regardless of nationality. The seasonal-worker E-8 created after December 2019 is not included.
Some agreement countries do not allow a refund — instead, your years in Korea are added to your home-country pension. Check your nationality on the National Pension Service (NPS) list or call 1355 before you leave.

How much you get back

The refund is the total contributions paid for you — employee and employer shares together — plus interest. In 2026 the combined rate is 9.5% of your monthly income.

  • Monthly income ₩3,000,000 × 9.5% = ₩285,000 paid into the pension each month (₩142,500 from you, ₩142,500 from your employer).
  • Two years at that level = about ₩6.84 million, plus interest.

How to claim

  1. Before you leave: visit an NPS branch up to one month before departure with your passport, alien registration card, flight ticket and bank account details. You can receive the money in your home-country account, or in a Korean account.
  2. At the airport: if you apply in advance at a branch, you can collect the refund in foreign currency at Incheon Airport on your departure day (not on weekends, public holidays or the last business day of the year).
  3. After you leave: you can still claim from abroad by post or through a Korean embassy, within five years.

Things to know

  • The refund is paid only once your departure is confirmed (or with proof you will leave within a month).
  • If you come back to work in Korea later, your earlier period is not counted again once refunded.
  • Health and employment insurance are separate and are not refunded.

FAQ

Can US citizens get a refund?

The Korea–US agreement counts your Korean years toward US Social Security instead of paying a refund, so US citizens generally do not get a lump sum. Check with NPS for your situation.

I am on an E-7 or F visa. Can I still get a refund?

It depends on your nationality: if your country has reciprocity or a refund-type agreement with Korea, yes. The visa-based route only covers E-8, E-9 and H-2.

Is the refund taxed?

The lump-sum refund itself is generally not subject to Korean income tax when paid on leaving the country, but rules can change, so check the statement NPS gives you.
