Unemployment Benefits in Korea for Foreigners: Who Is Eligible, How Much and How to Apply

Korea Calculators · Updated 2026-11-05

At a glance
  • Foreigners can receive unemployment benefits if they were covered by employment insurance — which depends on the visa.
  • You need 180 days of coverage in the 18 months before leaving, and you must have lost your job involuntarily.
  • In 2026 the benefit is ₩66,048–₩68,100 a day (8-hour workers) for 120–270 days, tax-free.

Eligibility by visa

VisaEmployment insurance (unemployment part)
F-2 (resident), F-5 (permanent), F-6 (marriage)Covered like Korean workers
E-9, H-2Covered for job training and stability programs; the unemployment benefit part generally requires enrollment on application
Other work visas (E-1 to E-7, F-4 and others)Generally covered only if you applied to join

If you are not sure whether you were enrolled, check your payslip for an employment insurance deduction (0.9% of pay) or ask the Employment Center. Rules for foreigners have changed several times, so confirm your status by calling 1350.

Other conditions

  • 180 days of insured work in the 18 months before your last day.
  • Involuntary job loss — layoff, contract ending, closure, recommended resignation. Quitting voluntarily usually doesn't qualify unless there was an accepted reason such as unpaid wages or harassment.
  • Able and willing to work, actively looking for a job, and legally allowed to work in Korea with your visa.

How much and for how long (2026)

  • Daily amount: 60% of your average daily wage, with a maximum of ₩68,100 and a minimum of ₩66,048 for an 8-hour worker. Because the range is narrow, most people get almost the same amount.
  • Duration: 120 to 270 days, depending on your age (under 50, or 50+/disabled) and how long you were insured.
  • Example: under 50, insured 1–3 years, monthly wage ₩3 million → ₩66,048 × 150 days = ₩9,907,200.

How to apply

  1. Make sure your employer has filed your loss-of-employment report with the correct reason.
  2. Register as a job seeker on Work24 (work24.go.kr) and complete the online orientation.
  3. Visit your local Employment Center with your alien registration card to apply for recognition.
  4. Report your job search activities on the scheduled dates to keep receiving payments.
Apply soon after leaving: benefits can only be paid within 12 months of your last day, even if days remain. Losing a job can also affect your stay, so speak with immigration (1345) as well.

FAQ

Will receiving benefits affect my visa?

Receiving unemployment benefits itself is not a problem for F-2, F-5 and F-6 holders. For employment-based visas, losing your job can start a limited period to find a new employer, so check with immigration (1345) early.

Can I get benefits if I leave Korea?

No. You must be in Korea, available for work and actively looking for a job while receiving benefits.

Is there help in English?

The Employment and Labor helpline 1350 offers interpretation in many languages. Some Employment Centers also have staff for foreign job seekers.
