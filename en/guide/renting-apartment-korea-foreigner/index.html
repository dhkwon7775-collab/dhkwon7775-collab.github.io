Renting an Apartment in Korea as a Foreigner: Deposit, Monthly Rent, Agent Fee and Contract Checklist

Korea Calculators · Updated 2026-11-19

At a glance
  • Most foreigners rent on wolse: a deposit (보증금) plus monthly rent (월세). Jeonse is a single large deposit with no monthly rent.
  • You pay a real estate agent fee of up to 0.3–0.6% of the deal amount (deposit + rent × 100 for wolse).
  • After moving in, file a residence change report within 15 days and get a fixed date (확정일자) on your contract — together they protect your deposit.
  • A normal lease is 2 years; you can ask to renew once for 2 more years, with a rent increase of at most 5%.

Prices vary a lot by area and building. On top of rent you usually pay a monthly maintenance fee (관리비) for shared areas and sometimes water or internet — ask what it includes. To compare a deposit with monthly rent, use the jeonse ↔ wolse calculator; for how jeonse works, read jeonse explained.

The real estate agent fee

Licensed agents (부동산 중개사무소) charge both the tenant and the landlord. The legal maximum for housing leases is 0.3–0.6% of the deal amount, with fixed ceilings for small deals. For wolse the deal amount is deposit + monthly rent × 100 (× 70 if that is under ₩50 million).

Agents registered as general taxpayers may add 10% VAT. Agree on the fee before you sign.

Before you sign: checklist

  1. Check the property registry (등기부등본) — the owner's name should match the landlord, and large mortgages (근저당) mean more risk to your deposit. You can view it online at iros.go.kr for a small fee.
  2. Pay only to the owner's account, never to a third person, and keep every transfer record.
  3. Read the special terms (특약) — who pays for repairs, cleaning, early move-out, and when the deposit is returned.
  4. Get a contract you can understand. The Ministry of Justice and Seoul Global Center offer standard lease contracts in English and other languages.
  5. Confirm the address exactly (building and unit number) as it appears in the registry.

After you move in: protect your deposit

  • Residence change report (체류지 변경신고) within 15 days at your local community center (주민센터) or online at HiKorea. For registered foreigners this counts as the move-in report Korean tenants file, so it gives you the same legal protection. Late filing can mean a fine.
  • Fixed date (확정일자) on your lease contract, at the community center or online. With the residence report and actual move-in, it puts you in line to get your deposit back first if the home is sold at auction.
  • For larger deposits, ask about deposit return insurance (전세보증금반환보증) from HUG or SGI; eligibility for foreigners depends on your residence status.
Do the residence report and the fixed date on the day you move in. Protection starts from the next day, so a mortgage registered on your move-in day can still rank ahead of you.

FAQ

Can foreigners rent an apartment in Korea?

Yes. You need your passport or alien registration card and money for the deposit. Some landlords prefer tenants with an alien registration card and a Korean bank account.

How much is the deposit for a one-room in Seoul?

For wolse it is commonly ₩5–20 million with monthly rent of ₩500,000–900,000, depending on the area and building. A higher deposit usually means lower monthly rent.

Do foreigners need to report a new address?

Yes. Registered foreigners must file a residence change report within 15 days of moving. It also gives your lease the same legal protection as a Korean tenant's move-in report.

Can the landlord raise the rent when I renew?

If you use your right to renew (one extra 2-year term), the increase is capped at 5%. A brand-new contract is not capped.
